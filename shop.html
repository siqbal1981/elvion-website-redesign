Choosing the right bulb

Three minutes of checking saves a returned part.

Replacement lamps for diagnostic instruments are not interchangeable. Two instrument heads can look identical on the wall and still take different lamps, so the model number — not the appearance — is what determines the right part.

01

Find your model number

The model or reference number is usually printed on the instrument head itself, or on the handle it attaches to. It's the number that identifies the device — not the number on the old bulb, which can be worn away or missing entirely.

02

Match the part, not the look

Lamps of similar size can differ in voltage and in how they seat in the housing. A lamp that physically fits is not automatically the correct lamp, which is why we list specific part numbers rather than describing bulbs by size or shape.

03

Confirm before ordering

Send us the device model and we'll confirm the correct part number before you buy. It takes one message, and it's considerably less trouble than returning a lamp that was never going to fit your instrument.

About the lamp

Why the lamp is the part worth getting right.

During an examination, a clinician is making judgements based on what they can see — colour, contrast, and the fine detail of tissue. That assessment depends on the light being the same today as it was last week. A lamp matched to the instrument's voltage and fitment is what makes that consistency possible.

Select the lamp by the instrument model and its required voltage; the listed references are not interchangeable. Packaging may vary; confirm the current package details for your selected ELVION model.
